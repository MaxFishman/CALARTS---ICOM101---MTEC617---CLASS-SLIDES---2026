> **Intro to Web Development · ICOM101 / MTEC617 · CalArts · Spring 2026**
> **Week 01 — What is the Web?**
> _Assignment_

## Overview

This week we asked a simple question: what is the web, and what makes a good website? Before we write a single line of code, it helps to learn how to look at the web the way a designer does. This first assignment has no coding at all. Instead, you'll visit real websites and pay close attention to how they're built, organized, and presented. Think of it as training your eye.

## What you'll practice

- Reading a website "like a designer" — noticing choices instead of just using the site.
- Describing what you like and dislike, and explaining *why*.
- Using a shared checklist to evaluate design, navigation, organization, interactivity, and responsiveness.
- Writing a clear, specific summary of your observations.

## The assignment

1. **Choose three websites to explore.** Pick from businesses, organizations, or personal blogs. Try to choose sites that feel different from one another so you have more to compare.
2. **Spend at least 5 minutes on each site.** As you browse, take notes on what you like and what you dislike. Don't rush — the goal is to really notice things, not just click around.
3. **Write a short summary analyzing those elements and why.** Explain what worked and what didn't for each site, and back up your points with specific examples (name the page, the menu, the button, the layout — whatever you're reacting to).

## While you explore, ask yourself

- What's the overall design? Is it visually appealing?
- Is the navigation intuitive — can you find things easily?
- Is the content organized in a logical way?
- Any interactive elements — forms, videos, games?
- Is it responsive to the size of the screen?

## What to submit

- The three website addresses you explored.
- Your written summary analyzing what you liked and disliked on each site, with specific examples that reference the checklist questions above.

## How it's graded

This is a light, exploratory assignment. Full credit comes from doing the work thoughtfully:

- **Three sites explored (businesses, organizations, or personal blogs)** — you visited three qualifying sites.
- **Time and attention** — you spent at least 5 minutes on each and noted what you liked and disliked.
- **Analysis with specific examples** — your summary explains the *why* behind your reactions and points to concrete details rather than staying vague.
- **Uses the checklist** — your notes engage with the design, navigation, organization, interactivity, and responsiveness questions.
